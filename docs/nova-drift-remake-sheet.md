# Nova Drift Remake Sheet

A 2–3 phase remake plan for an EV Nova-style game concept to serve as the next major build target inside the arcade repo.

## Working Title
**Nova Drift**

Alternate names:
- Starhaul
- Dead Reckoning
- Outer Freight
- Blackflag Sector

## Core Promise
Start as a tiny independent captain in a living galaxy. Haul cargo, take jobs, hunt bounties, explore dangerous systems, disable and board ships, steal or capture them, and slowly become a fleet-owning force that can tilt wars and economies.

## North Star
**Freedom first. Progression second. Story through consequence.**

This should feel like:
- I can go anywhere
- Every ship upgrade matters
- I made my own story
- I should NOT have won that fight, but now I own their ship

## Genre
Open-galaxy space sandbox  
Action-RPG + trading sim + tactical fleet lite

## Player Fantasy Pillars
1. **Scrappy captain rise**  
   Go from rust-bucket nobody to feared operator.

2. **Capture and command**  
   Disable ships, board them, steal cargo, claim hulls, grow a fleet.

3. **Live by your choices**  
   Trader, pirate, merc, courier, rebel, loyalist, explorer.

4. **Galaxy with gravity**  
   Factions shift, routes change, wars open and close opportunities.

---

## Phase 1 — The “It Already Rules” Version

### Goal
Ship the cleanest modern playable core. Get the toy fun online fast.

### Scope
- Singleplayer only
- 2D or 2.5D top-down
- Stylized clean UI
- 25–40 systems
- 4–6 factions
- 25–40 ship hulls
- Core economy and mission loop
- One major storyline + procedural contracts

### Must-Have Systems
- Real-time ship flight and combat
- Hyperspace / jump travel
- Trading and cargo market
- Ship outfitting
- Mission boards
- Faction reputation
- Disable + board + loot
- Ship capture and ownership
- Escorts / small fleet following
- Starmap exploration fog
- Pirate, patrol, trader traffic simulation
- Docking, repair, refuel, rearm

### Core Loop
Take job -> travel -> trade / fight / scavenge -> upgrade ship -> unlock harder sectors -> capture bigger hulls -> widen reputation and options

### “This Is the Hook” Moments
- Your first profitable cargo run
- Your first near-death pirate fight
- Your first successful boarding action
- Your first captured ship
- Your first two-ship escort fleet
- Your first faction choice with consequences

### Content Target
Enough content for players to get properly lost:
- Starter region
- Frontier region
- Pirate-heavy border
- High-security core
- Weird unknown edge pocket

### Art Direction
Not photoreal. Clean, readable, slightly painterly sci-fi UI. Ships should have strong silhouettes and faction identity. Space should feel lonely, dangerous, and big, not just neon wallpaper.

### UI Tone
- Less spreadsheet than EVE
- Less noisy than Starsector
- More tactile than old EV
- Fast, legible, elegant

### Success Metric
People say:

> “I meant to do one cargo run and suddenly it was 2am and I had stolen a corvette.”

## Phase 2 — The “Living Galaxy” Version

### Goal
Turn a great sandbox into a world that pushes back.

### Added Systems
- Dynamic economy with regional shortages and surpluses
- Faction wars that shift lanes and patrol levels
- Station ownership / blockade states
- Deeper ship crew and boarding risks
- Persistent captains and rivals
- Smuggling / contraband / scanning
- Distress calls and emergent encounters
- Salvage fields after major battles
- Insurance, permits, bounties, and wanted levels
- More robust fleet commands

### Storyline Expansion
Add 3–5 major branching arcs:
- Frontier rebellion
- Corporate extraction war
- Pirate coalition rising
- Alien / precursor mystery edge campaign
- Government black ops thread

Each arc should:
- Begin with humble jobs
- Fork midway
- Lock / unlock sectors or ship access
- Meaningfully reshape travel safety and markets

### Player Identity Grows
By this phase, the player is not just "a pilot." They are becoming:
- A trade magnate
- A mercenary commander
- A feared pirate lord
- A smuggler king
- A loyal faction war asset
- A free agent poking every hornet nest

### Diplomacy and Consequence
Faction standing should matter hard:
- Docking restrictions
- Military patrol hostility
- Black market access
- Special ship licenses
- Escort recruitment
- Storyline branch locks

### Success Metric
Players tell stories that sound handcrafted, even though the systems caused them.

---

## Phase 3 — The “Forever Game” Version

### Goal
Make it the game people inhabit for years.

### Added Systems
- Co-op or async shared galaxy layer
- Mod tools / scenario editor
- Custom factions and ship packs
- Territory influence and sector control
- Capital ships and carrier gameplay
- Colony / station contracts or sponsorship
- Advanced fleet formations and commands
- Seasonal galaxy events
- Faction election / leadership / coups
- Endgame threats beyond normal warfare

### Multiplayer Direction
Do **not** make it an MMO. That would kill the charm.

Better directions:
- 2–4 player drop-in co-op
- Shared campaign galaxy
- Optional async economy / bounty board
- Community scenarios and modded maps

### Creator Layer
This genre lives forever if people can make:
- Ships
- Factions
- Mission chains
- Total conversion galaxies
- Weird edge-case stories

This is where the old Ambrosia magic comes back in a modern way.

### Success Metric
People do not just finish it. They settle into careers inside it.

## Combat Design Notes

### Combat Should Be
- Readable
- Lethal enough to be exciting
- Not a bullet-hell mess
- More about loadout, timing, angle, and risk than twitch alone

### Boarding Should Feel Like
A deliciously bad idea that sometimes changes your life.

Simple version:
- Disable ship
- Match / latch
- Boarding odds shown clearly
- Risk crew loss / hull damage / trap events
- Outcomes: loot only, partial control, full capture, failed retreat

Later version:
- Crew specialties
- Ship security modules
- Morale and surrender states
- Captain traits

---

## Economy Design Notes

### The economy should support fantasy, not become homework
Players should be able to:
- Do simple profitable freight runs
- Spot route patterns
- Exploit war shortages
- Smuggle high-margin illegal goods
- Salvage battlefields for opportunistic cash

Avoid:
- Too much market opacity
- Giant spreadsheet burden
- Fake complexity with tiny payoff

## Ship Class Ladder

### Rough Progression Fantasy
- Shuttle / courier
- Light freighter
- Interceptor
- Gunship
- Heavy freighter
- Corvette
- Destroyer
- Cruiser / carrier-lite
- Rare faction capital-adjacent monster

Important: capturing a ship should sometimes let players “skip the ladder” in a thrilling but dangerous way.

---

## Faction Set
You want instantly legible power blocs:

1. **Central authority** — stable, bureaucratic, militarized, lawful
2. **Frontier independents** — scrappy, practical, freedom-minded
3. **Corporations** — wealthy, extractive, logistics monsters
4. **Pirates / raiders** — predatory, fragmented, opportunistic
5. **Cult / ideological bloc** — strange tech, weird motives, high flavor
6. **Unknown edge faction** — late-game mystery energy

---

## Visual Tone Refs
Not copying these, just vibe lanes:
- Starsector for fleet tension
- FTL for elegant clarity
- Rebel Galaxy for blue-collar space mood
- Elite / Privateer lineage for career fantasy
- Homeworld UI discipline for restraint

---

## What Not to Screw Up
1. Do not overmodernize the soul away — the charm is freedom, not cinematic railroading.
2. Do not make onboarding miserable — old-school mystery is good; old-school friction is not.
3. Do not bury boarding/capture — that is one of the signature joys.
4. Do not make fleets too fiddly too early — start lean, deepen later.
5. Do not turn the economy into accounting cosplay — enough numbers to matter, not enough to require a CPA.
