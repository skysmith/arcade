# Jumpwake Post-Empire Bible

Expansion sketch for a larger `Jumpwake` setting built on post-imperial logistics, vacant planetoids, inherited megastructures, and small captains living inside old civilizational leftovers.

This does not assume magical abundance.
It assumes that huge things were built, many of them outlived the powers that funded them, and now ordinary people survive by maintaining, scavenging, routing around, or quietly exploiting them.

## Core Premise

The nearby lanes are not the beginning of space civilization.
They are the maintenance shadow of something larger.

The old empire did not disappear cleanly.
It thinned.
It consolidated.
It rewrote maps.
It turned some worlds into civic centers, some into tolerated dependencies, and some into infrastructural ghosts that still matter even when nobody wants to admit it.

That means:

- a vacant planetoid can still have active docking law
- a dead ring can still define trade geometry
- a relay spine can outlast the ministry that named it
- a salvage crew can recover politically dangerous memory from industrial wreckage

## Megastructure Layer

Use Isaac Arthur scale as background pressure, not constant foreground spectacle.

The player should feel:

- they are flying through service volumes, edge habitats, relay corridors, abandoned shellworks, dismantled ring sections, and downgraded industrial systems
- the grandest structures are mostly too large to comprehend from one cockpit
- what matters to local life is not who built them, but who still has access, spare parts, and inherited legitimacy

### Good Megastructure Rules For Jumpwake

- Keep the current sector map practical and human-sized.
- Let megastructures appear as neighboring systems, distant silhouettes, graveyards, and specialized mission zones.
- Tie every huge structure to one grounded question:
  - who owns it now
  - who maintains it
  - who is locked out
  - what traffic it still forces through local lanes

## New Character Set

These should feel useful, compromised, and immediately gameable.

### 1. Asha Venn

- `Role`: debt auditor for habitat maintenance bonds
- `Voice`: precise, dry, quietly predatory
- `Use`: exposes the financial skeleton under “independent” stations
- `Want`: access to station ledgers, freight shortfalls, and hidden subsidy trails
- `Secret`: she knows several frontier ports are only alive because default was politically delayed

### 2. Brother Coil

- `Role`: monk-engineer from an order that preserves spin habitats
- `Voice`: gentle, patient, doctrinal in a way that becomes eerie
- `Use`: brings sacred maintenance culture into the setting
- `Want`: rare parts, structural scans, and captains willing to enter dangerous old habitats respectfully
- `Secret`: his order keeps archives other governments would call unauthorized sovereignty records

### 3. Pella Rook

- `Role`: tug captain from a dismantling guild
- `Voice`: blunt, funny, impossible to impress
- `Use`: connects salvage, megastructure decay, and heavy-industry labor
- `Want`: old couplings, obsolete lattice joints, and crews who can tow without asking poetic questions
- `Secret`: she is stripping one dead imperial project to keep three frontier stations alive

### 4. Orin Glass

- `Role`: envoy from a wealthy cylinder polity
- `Voice`: cultured, curious, slightly horrified by frontier informality
- `Use`: gives us a surviving high-function society that is neither pure villain nor pure solution
- `Want`: escorts, local intermediaries, and plausible deniability outside formal lanes
- `Secret`: his polity depends on frontier raw material flows more than its own etiquette admits

### 5. Sera Quill

- `Role`: black-market cartographer
- `Voice`: restless, lucid, conspiratorial
- `Use`: opens weird routes and dead industrial space
- `Want`: updated beacon maps, traffic anomalies, and sensor logs from places insurers call myth
- `Secret`: she is reconstructing unofficial navigation through systems removed from public route maps

### 6. Eamon Vale

- `Role`: keeper of a vacant planetoid customs office
- `Voice`: ceremonial, lonely, deeply stubborn
- `Use`: makes abandoned places feel bureaucratically alive
- `Want`: visiting captains to honor obsolete docking procedure because procedure is the only thing left
- `Secret`: he knows his office exists only because somebody still routes something hidden through that rock

### 7. Lio Kes

- `Role`: mirror-field pilot from a solar collection swarm
- `Voice`: fast, technical, always sounding half sunstruck
- `Use`: introduces energy infrastructure scale without turning the game into hard-sf homework
- `Want`: shipments, emergency thermal dumps, and crews willing to brave glare-heavy work zones
- `Secret`: the collectors are being quietly repurposed for transmission work nobody local approved

### 8. Matra Seln

- `Role`: archivist-salvager specializing in sealed habitat records
- `Voice`: reverent, greedy, emotionally contained
- `Use`: bridges Dax-style salvage with post-imperial memory politics
- `Want`: black-box cores, resident registries, and internal habitat recordings
- `Secret`: she sells memory twice, once to historians and once to the people afraid of historians

## New Places

These should feel like playable destinations, rumor engines, and image prompts.

### 1. Halo Mile

- `Type`: broken orbital ring segment
- `Use`: market, shrine, pirate corridor, salvage economy
- `Hook`: everyone calls it a ruin, but it still dictates approach geometry for half a system

### 2. Saint Radiant

- `Type`: failing Bishop-ring habitat
- `Use`: agriculture, politics, evacuation pressure, class tension
- `Hook`: its interior still feeds stations that publicly pretend they are not dependent on it

### 3. Choir Array

- `Type`: abandoned statite cluster
- `Use`: weird traffic, smuggler caches, acoustic superstition
- `Hook`: thermal stress makes the platforms “sing,” and somebody is using the empty mirrors for covert signaling

### 4. Vanta Reach

- `Type`: dark industrial collector swarm
- `Use`: energy economy, private routing, dangerous courier work
- `Hook`: the safest route through it belongs to no government willing to admit ownership

### 5. Kettle Spine

- `Type`: chain of welded refinery habitats
- `Use`: labor politics, fuel trade, rough hospitality
- `Hook`: what began as temporary industrial linkage has become a culture and a polity

### 6. Ninth Berth

- `Type`: maybe-real hidden logistics channel
- `Use`: mystery thread, broker lore, overlapping institutional secrecy
- `Hook`: everyone important recognizes the phrase and none of them define it the same way

### 7. Glasswake

- `Type`: vacant planetoid customs and fuel stop
- `Use`: lonely law, ghost bureaucracy, quiet smuggling
- `Hook`: nearly abandoned, still stamped, still logged, still politically inconvenient

### 8. The Long Spindle

- `Type`: topopole-linked transfer chain between minor habitats
- `Use`: delicate freight, maintenance missions, structural terror
- `Hook`: the route exists because rebuilding something safer would require admitting how much traffic still depends on it

## Megastructure Remnants

These are the big-background concepts that can feed many missions.

### 1. The Dimming Crown

- `Type`: partial orbital ring around a stripped world
- `Status`: broken into inhabited, forbidden, and scavenged sections
- `Narrative Value`: former imperial confidence turned local gray economy

### 2. The Quiet Cylinder Compact

- `Type`: cluster of surviving O'Neill-cylinder polities
- `Status`: rich, functional, politically careful
- `Narrative Value`: proves that parts of the old order still work, just not for everyone

### 3. Red Choir Mirrors

- `Type`: repurposed solar or transmission mirrors
- `Status`: publicly industrial, privately strategic
- `Narrative Value`: lets energy infrastructure become intelligence infrastructure

### 4. The Hollow Ledger

- `Type`: mined-out planetoid with embedded archive vaults
- `Status`: mostly empty, still logged, legally messy
- `Narrative Value`: ideal salvage-history zone

### 5. Pilgrim Bone

- `Type`: dead migration habitat under tow for generations
- `Status`: drifting shrine, black market, refugee memory site
- `Narrative Value`: emotionally charged place where logistics and grief are inseparable

### 6. The Velvet Mass

- `Type`: dark industrial mass-hub around an old transport nexus
- `Status`: not abandoned, not lawful, too useful to shut down
- `Narrative Value`: where broker power starts looking indistinguishable from sovereignty

## Tone Rules

For post-empire `Jumpwake`, keep these truths visible:

- big structures survive longer than the societies that justified them
- infrastructure creates local culture
- abandonment is rarely total; it is usually selective
- even dead systems still have maintenance religions, legal residue, scavenger maps, and inherited class structures
- a lonely berth on a vacant rock can matter because something enormous still routes through it indirectly

## North Star

`Jumpwake` should feel like a game where small crews live inside giant leftovers, and every freight run, salvage pull, permit dispute, and private message hints that the old empire never really ended.
It just stopped paying for all of itself at once.
