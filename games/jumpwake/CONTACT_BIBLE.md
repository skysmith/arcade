# Jumpwake Contact Bible

Working narrative spine for the in-port inbox/chat presentation.

This is not a full plot outline.
It is a pressure map for recurring contacts so we can keep the game tense, surprising, and coherent while writing only a few beats ahead.

## Story Rules

- The player should rarely know the full shape of a thread.
- Early jobs should look practical before they reveal deeper stakes.
- Contacts should want different things from the player.
- Threads can brush against each other, but they should not all collapse into one master plot too early.
- Every contact should feel useful, dangerous, needy, or magnetic within 1-2 messages.
- We should usually know the next 2 beats, not the final ending.

## World Pressure

These are the background forces every contact can lean on:

- `Central Authority` wants regulated lanes, registries, permits, and political obedience.
- `Frontier Union` wants working systems to survive without waiting for core approval.
- `Independents` want room to hustle, survive, and avoid being folded into somebody else's order.
- `Nightglass Syndicate` wants deniable movement, quiet leverage, and trusted couriers.
- `Dust Pirates` are less a government than a weather pattern made of hunger and opportunism.

## Contact Template

For each recurring contact we only need to know:

- `Voice`: how they sound in chat
- `Want`: what they need from the player
- `Secret`: what they are not saying yet
- `Escalation`: what kind of pressure their thread grows into
- `Crossovers`: who they can naturally intersect with

## Core Cast

### Mara Kade

- `Role`: Grey Exchange regular, fixer, reader of people
- `Voice`: dry, observant, intimate without being warm
- `Surface Want`: reliable runners who can carry freight and quiet packets without panicking
- `Secret`: she is slowly rebuilding an informal independent network that still answers Grey Exchange before it answers anyone official
- `Escalation`: “small haul” becomes “who still answers the station when bigger powers lean on it”
- `Crossover`: Juno, Vey, Dax
- `Use`: good first recurring contact because she can introduce the player to the world without overexplaining it

First beats:

1. `Mara's Quiet Haul`
Looks like a simple competence test.
Really asks: do you come back when you said you would?

2. `Mara's Dockside Whisper`
Quiet packet, quiet return.
Reveals that the freight was never the important part.

3. `Mara's Open Door`
An introduction outward.
This is where she starts turning the player into somebody other people call.

Questions to keep open:

- Is Mara protecting Grey Exchange from the Authority, Nightglass, or both?
- Does she actually believe in independence, or just in surviving between larger predators?

### Dax Brindle

- `Role`: wreck diver, salvage obsessive, practical historian
- `Voice`: amused, macabre, mechanically precise
- `Surface Want`: somebody who can recover hulls and fragments without treating every wreck like scrap
- `Secret`: he notices patterns in lost ships and missing manifests that point to a larger hidden history
- `Escalation`: salvage work becomes memory work, then political archaeology
- `Crossover`: Barnard's Star, core relay history, Mara, Authority clerks
- `Use`: lets us reveal setting history through physical recovery instead of exposition

First beats:

1. `Dax's Cinder Sweep`
Basic salvage credibility test.

2. `Dax's Ledger Fragment`
A wreck contains paper that should matter to someone.

3. `Dax's Red Ledger`
The salvage line opens onto old missing traffic and forgotten obligations.

Questions to keep open:

- Were those ships lost by accident, corruption, or deliberate erasure?
- Is Dax chasing truth, closure, profit, or revenge?

### Juno Vale

- `Role`: Frontier Union dispatcher, labor pragmatist, convoy organizer
- `Voice`: clear, direct, emotionally contained until pushed
- `Surface Want`: medicine, supplies, proof, and captains who do not disappear when a route gets ugly
- `Secret`: she is trying to turn local outrage into durable policy, not just one more temporary fix
- `Escalation`: relief work becomes evidence gathering, then political leverage
- `Crossover`: Alpha Centauri freight houses, Authority permit structure, Mara
- `Use`: grounds the setting in ordinary people who actually need the lane to function

First beats:

1. `Juno's Relief Drop`
Urgent, human, practical.

2. `Juno's Convoy Proof`
Shifts from supply to blame.

3. `Juno's Union Voice`
Turns paperwork and proof into a pressure campaign.

Questions to keep open:

- How far will Juno bend procedure to get people what they need?
- Does she want reform, autonomy, or a full break from core dependence?

### Clerk Ilya Sen

- `Role`: Registry liaison, clean-handed face of the core
- `Voice`: clipped, exact, occasionally dry in a way that almost becomes funny
- `Surface Want`: trusted civilians who can move sealed packets and carry official ambiguity
- `Secret`: he knows the core is weaker and more dependent on local networks than it likes to admit
- `Escalation`: permit work becomes institutional embarrassment and quiet power struggle
- `Crossover`: Sol arc, Dax's records, Juno's documentation
- `Use`: gives the core a human face that is not cartoon-villain simple

First beats:

1. `Core Transit Screening`
Looks procedural.
Actually marks the player as somebody the system now notices.

2. `Old Light Charter`
Core paperwork reaches farther than expected.

3. `Blue Archive Hearing`
The player becomes part of a real policy fight without ever being in charge of it.

Questions to keep open:

- Is Ilya trying to protect the system, reform it, or cover for it?
- How much of his honesty is strategic?

### Vey Neral

- `Role`: Nightglass broker
- `Voice`: elegant, insinuating, always one layer ahead
- `Surface Want`: quiet transport, deniable movement, selective loyalty
- `Secret`: Vey measures people by how they carry secrets, not by what they say
- `Escalation`: simple smuggling becomes trust, then leverage, then emotional debt
- `Crossover`: Mara, Mirage Verge, Tau Ceti, Authority scanners
- `Use`: gives gray routes a seductive and unnerving center

First beats:

1. `Nightglass Offer`
A side choice with more meaning than it first appears.

2. `Quiet Wake`
Tests discipline under pressure.

3. `Burn Notice`
Reveals that the player is no longer just moving packets, but participating in narrative misdirection.

Questions to keep open:

- Does Vey ever tell the whole truth?
- Is Nightglass preserving alternatives to Authority order, or simply profiting from fracture?

### Marshal Tovin

- `Role`: lane marshal, bounty issuer, not-quite-clean law
- `Voice`: terse, tired, still capable of anger
- `Surface Want`: captains who can reduce pirate pressure without creating worse chaos
- `Secret`: some “lawful” violence is just politics with cleaner signatures
- `Escalation`: bounty work becomes dirty enforcement
- `Crossover`: Authority, pirates, Juno
- `Use`: lets combat jobs reveal moral compromise rather than just target practice

Potential first beats:

1. disable raiders
2. identify who benefits from raids
3. decide whether an officially sanctioned target is actually guilty

## Overlap Map

These are the useful partial intersections:

- `Mara <-> Juno`
Independent survival vs organized frontier resilience

- `Dax <-> Ilya`
Recovered records vs official records

- `Juno <-> Ilya`
human need vs procedural legitimacy

- `Mara <-> Vey`
parallel informal networks with very different ethics

- `Dax <-> Sol arc`
history recovered from wreckage can alter present politics

Not every thread needs to merge.
Some should remain local and personal.

## Reveal Strategy

Keep reveals in this order:

1. `Need`
Why does this contact need the player right now?

2. `Pattern`
Why does this job feel like it belongs to something bigger?

3. `Cost`
What happens if the player keeps helping this person?

4. `Contradiction`
What about this contact does not line up cleanly?

5. `Choice`
What kind of player does this thread ask you to become?

## Writing Tone

- Keep messages short enough to read on a controller.
- Avoid lore-dump paragraphs.
- Let subtext do the heavy lifting.
- Prefer “specific and suggestive” over “comprehensive and explanatory.”
- End many messages with a line that creates motion, suspicion, or emotional residue.

## Practical Rule For New Threads

When adding a new contact:

1. Give them a distinct voice in 2 lines or less.
2. Give them a want that can produce practical missions.
3. Give them one hidden truth.
4. Write only the first 2 beats in detail.
5. Sketch 2 possible later turns, but do not lock them.

That keeps the game surprising while still feeling authored.
