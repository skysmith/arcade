# Whispering Willow Contact Bible

Working narrative spine for the in-quay inbox/chat presentation.

This is not a full plot outline.
It is a pressure map for recurring contacts so we can keep the game tense, surprising, and coherent while writing only a few beats ahead.

## Story Rules

- The player should rarely know the full shape of a thread.
- Early jobs should look practical before they reveal deeper stakes.
- Contacts should want different things from the player.
- Threads can brush against each other, but they should not all collapse into one master plot too early.
- Every contact should feel useful, dangerous, needy, or magnetic within 1-2 messages.
- We should usually know the next 2 beats, not the final ending.

## World Pressure

These are the background forces every contact can lean on:

- `Wardens` want regulated crossings, ledgers, path tokens, and political obedience.
- `Hearthfolk` want working systems to survive without waiting for Sunroot approval.
- `Wayfolk` want room to hustle, survive, and avoid being folded into somebody else's order.
- `Moonglass Circle` wants deniable movement, quiet leverage, and trusted couriers.
- `Goblin Clans and hidden coves` are not a single enemy; they are frightened families, raiders, and opportunists crowded into the same shadowed routes.

## Contact Template

For each recurring contact we only need to know:

- `Voice`: how they sound in chat
- `Want`: what they need from the player
- `Secret`: what they are not saying yet
- `Escalation`: what kind of pressure their thread grows into
- `Crossovers`: who they can naturally intersect with

## Core Cast

### Auntie Sable Marris

- `Role`: Mangrove House host, fixer, reader of people
- `Voice`: dry, observant, intimate without losing practicality
- `Surface Want`: reliable runners who can carry bundles and quiet packets without panicking
- `Secret`: she is slowly rebuilding an informal Wayfolk network that still answers Mangrove House before it answers anyone official
- `Escalation`: “small errand” becomes “who still answers the house when bigger powers lean on it”
- `Crossover`: Nera, the hooded figure, Pip
- `Use`: good first recurring contact because she can introduce the player to the world without overexplaining it

First beats:

1. `Sable's Quiet Errand`
Looks like a simple competence test.
Really asks: do you come back when you said you would?

2. `Sable's Quiet Whisper`
Quiet packet, quiet return.
Reveals that the bundles were never the important part.

3. `Sable's Open Door`
An introduction outward.
This is where she starts turning the player into somebody other people call.

Questions to keep open:

- Is Sable protecting Mangrove House from the Wardens, Moonglass, or both?
- Does she actually believe in independence, or just in surviving between larger powers?

### Pip Tidewell

- `Role`: reef gleaner, recovery obsessive, practical historian
- `Voice`: amused, macabre, mechanically precise
- `Surface Want`: somebody who can recover boat fragments without treating every wreck like scrap
- `Secret`: he notices patterns in lost ferries and missing ledgers that point to a larger hidden history
- `Escalation`: recovery work becomes memory work, then political archaeology
- `Crossover`: Driftwood Rest, old tide-law history, Sable, Warden clerks
- `Use`: lets us reveal setting history through physical recovery instead of exposition

First beats:

1. `Pip's Fen Sweep`
Basic recovery credibility test.

2. `Pip's Ledger Fragment`
A wreck contains paper that should matter to someone.

3. `Pip's Red Ledger`
The recovery line opens onto old missing crossings and forgotten obligations.

Questions to keep open:

- Were those ferries lost by accident, corruption, or deliberate erasure?
- Is Pip chasing truth, closure, profit, or revenge?

### Nera Voss

- `Role`: Hearthfolk healer, labor pragmatist, crossing organizer
- `Voice`: clear, direct, emotionally contained until pushed
- `Surface Want`: medicine, supplies, proof, and travelers who do not disappear when a route gets ugly
- `Secret`: she is trying to turn local outrage into durable policy, not just one more temporary fix
- `Escalation`: relief work becomes evidence gathering, then political leverage
- `Crossover`: Reefspan ferry houses, Warden path-token structure, Sable
- `Use`: grounds the setting in ordinary people who actually need the crossings to function

First beats:

1. `Nera's Relief Drop`
Urgent, human, practical.

2. `Nera's Proof Packet`
Shifts from supply to blame.

3. `Nera's Banyan Voice`
Turns paperwork and proof into a pressure campaign.

Questions to keep open:

- How far will Nera bend procedure to get people what they need?
- Does she want reform, autonomy, or a full break from Sunroot dependence?

### Warden Elric Sen

- `Role`: Warden ledger liaison, clean-handed face of old tide law
- `Voice`: clipped, exact, occasionally dry in a way that almost becomes funny
- `Surface Want`: trusted civilians who can move sealed packets and carry official ambiguity
- `Secret`: he knows Sunroot is weaker and more dependent on local networks than it likes to admit
- `Escalation`: path-token work becomes institutional embarrassment and quiet power struggle
- `Crossover`: Sunroot arc, Pip's records, Nera's documentation
- `Use`: gives the Wardens a human face that is not cartoon-villain simple

First beats:

1. `Deep Path Blessing`
Looks procedural.
Actually marks the player as somebody the system now notices.

2. `Old Tide Charter`
Sunroot paperwork reaches farther than expected.

3. `Lagoon Archive Hearing`
The player becomes part of a real policy fight without ever being in charge of it.

Questions to keep open:

- Is Elric trying to protect the system, reform it, or cover for it?
- How much of his honesty is strategic?

### The Hooded Figure

- `Role`: Moonglass broker
- `Voice`: elegant, insinuating, always one layer ahead
- `Surface Want`: quiet transport, deniable movement, selective loyalty
- `Secret`: the hooded figure measures people by how they carry secrets, not by what they say
- `Escalation`: simple smuggling becomes trust, then leverage, then emotional debt
- `Crossover`: Sable, Moonglass Cay, Quiet Keys, Warden watchers
- `Use`: gives hidden routes a seductive and unnerving center

First beats:

1. `Moonglass Offer`
A side choice with more meaning than it first appears.

2. `Quiet Wake`
Tests discipline under pressure.

3. `Burn Notice`
Reveals that the player is no longer just moving packets, but participating in narrative misdirection.

Questions to keep open:

- Does the hooded figure ever tell the whole truth?
- Is Moonglass preserving alternatives to Warden order, or simply profiting from fracture?

### Bramble Kade

- `Role`: path warden, trouble-board issuer, not-quite-clean law
- `Voice`: terse, tired, still capable of anger
- `Surface Want`: travelers who can reduce raider pressure without creating worse chaos
- `Secret`: some “lawful” violence is just politics with cleaner signatures
- `Escalation`: safety work becomes dirty enforcement
- `Crossover`: Wardens, goblin coves, Nera
- `Use`: lets combat jobs reveal moral compromise rather than just target practice

Potential first beats:

1. calm or disable raiders
2. identify who benefits from raids
3. decide whether an officially sanctioned target is actually guilty

## Overlap Map

These are the useful partial intersections:

- `Sable <-> Nera`
Wayfolk survival vs organized Hearthfolk resilience

- `Pip <-> Elric`
Recovered records vs official records

- `Nera <-> Elric`
human need vs procedural legitimacy

- `Sable <-> Hooded Figure`
parallel informal networks with very different ethics

- `Pip <-> Sunroot arc`
history recovered from wreckage can alter present politics

Not every thread needs to merge.
Some should remain local and personal.

## Reveal Strategy

Keep reveals in this order:

1. `Need`
Why does this contact need the player right now?

2. `Pattern`
Why does this job feel like it belongs to something bigger?

3. `Cost`
What happens if the player keeps helping this person?

4. `Contradiction`
What about this contact does not line up cleanly?

5. `Choice`
What kind of player does this thread ask you to become?

## Writing Tone

- Keep messages short enough to read on a controller.
- Avoid lore-dump paragraphs.
- Let subtext do the heavy lifting.
- Prefer “specific and suggestive” over “comprehensive and explanatory.”
- End many messages with a line that creates motion, suspicion, or emotional residue.

## Practical Rule For New Threads

When adding a new contact:

1. Give them a distinct voice in 2 lines or less.
2. Give them a want that can produce practical missions.
3. Give them one hidden truth.
4. Write only the first 2 beats in detail.
5. Sketch 2 possible later turns, but do not lock them.

That keeps the game surprising while still feeling authored.
